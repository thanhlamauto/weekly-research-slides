# Writing for listening

Narration is written before animation and reviewed together with the visual
beats. It is not a transcript of a finished video, and it is not paper prose.

## Core principle

**Animation shows WHAT changes. Narration explains WHY it matters.**

Bad:

> As shown, the trajectory is divided into high-noise, middle-noise, and
> low-noise stages.

Good:

> The feature does not evolve the same way throughout diffusion. Early changes
> are large. In the middle they become much smoother. Near the end, the model
> mostly refines details.

## Content standard (required)

> When present or explain anything, Our presentation must follow a very coherent structure that answer sequentially these question: Why we need to do this/The motivation? What are we going to do after having the requirement? Then explain what we do. You must explain very clear on what we are going to do, then present equation or proposition, theorem when we need clear formal formula to avoid vague, excessive wording. Then present experiment to support our claim if we had. The presentation must be very clear, concise, precise and connected as a complete, convincing story flow throughout the whole paper and each section, each subsection should have their own complete, convincing flow that become a perfectly fit part in the whole complete story flow of the paper. Every formula must be written in clear and easiest way to understand, all notation must be explained clearly before use. The presentation quality must be high, oral-standard, human-like with zero AI slop paragraph that throw to reader a bunch of words without truly saying anything meaningful. The complex mathematical theorem we introduce must be state clearly the message it is trying to deliver, no vague terms or jargon.

## Rules

- short sentences (aim <= 25 words);
- introduce a term before you use its symbol;
- no paper prose ("as shown", "we propose", "respectively", "thereby");
- do not read equations aloud mechanically — say what they mean;
- concrete before abstract;
- do not describe what is already obvious on screen;
- state causal links and why each transition matters;
- one new concept per beat;
- end a scene with a takeaway the viewer can repeat.

## Audience

- **expert** — field terminology is fine; equations early.
- **adjacent-researcher** (default for weekly research) — assume ML knowledge,
  but not this specific research area. Explain domain terms, motivations and
  hidden assumptions.
- **general-technical** — intuition before notation. Keep the science correct.

Never remove scientific correctness to simplify.

## Per-beat checklist

1. What is introduced? (object or term)
2. What changes on screen?
3. Why does it matter?
4. Does the viewer get a moment to read the result?
